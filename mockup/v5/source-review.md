## Evidence and limits

| Evidence | How to describe it |
| --- | --- |
| Research discovery | One fresh local run returned six recommendations in 77.5 seconds. Review covered record references, full dates, status and participation. Fit was an assessment. |
| Company enrichment | One 16-step editable Workflow Studio run produced a 3,021-word report in 101.4 seconds. Persisted record and index checks matched. Five short fields missed length targets. |
| Buyer screening | A test index held 521 profiles. A subsequent run scored 20 and saved ten ranked results in about 235 seconds. Two records carried eligibility warnings and needed analyst review. |
| Approval and reload | One local approved mutation completed and matched readback on a sample record. The journal remained visible after reload in that test. |
| Order planning, warehouse and receivables | Illustrative scenarios with sample data. Preserve the assumptions beside their sample outputs and explain the review authority. |
| ~33 hours per month | Baseline illustration calculated as 500 × 4 ÷ 60. This quantifies the assumed manual work for that scenario. |
| 100 vs 60 minutes | Existing website model. The assisted total includes 8 minutes of review. The 40-minute difference measures human effort only within the example. |

Performance results above are dated observations for specific configurations and inputs. Keep any public evidence near its source, run conditions and important limitations. Check publication permission before using customer names or company-specific screenshots. Keep the customer logo strip separate from execution evidence.

The Vietnamese slide also cites 9 hours per week finding information in Asana's knowledge-worker survey, a 0.34% per-field rate in a clinical single-entry study, and 47% of FP&A time collecting and validating data in the FP&A Trends survey. Each describes a different sample, measure and context. The homepage retains these three examples with their sources and treats the 500-record calculation as a hypothetical baseline.
