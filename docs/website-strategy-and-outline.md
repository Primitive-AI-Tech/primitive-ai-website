# Primitive AI website — revised strategy and outline

## 1. The central proposition

**Put AI to work across your organization.**

Core Agentic helps organizations become more productive, responsive, accurate, and transparent through AI agents connected to their existing knowledge, software, and workflows.

The business ambition is clear:

**Complete more work, reduce avoidable errors, respond faster, and lower the cost of delivering an accepted result.**

The website should develop that argument through this sequence:

**What we do → Why it matters → Productivity and economics → Practical applications → How we work with customers → What powers the solution → Why Primitive AI → Start a conversation.**

Each section should answer the question raised by the previous one.

| Visitor’s question | Website’s answer |
|---|---|
| What is this? | AI agents connected to organizational work |
| Why would we need it? | Too much capacity is consumed by manual coordination across systems |
| What could we gain? | More output, faster response, greater consistency, and better unit economics |
| Where would we use it? | Concrete sales, research, and internal operations workflows |
| How would we start? | A focused working engagement around a valuable problem |
| Can we trust it? | Approved knowledge, bounded authority, human oversight, and visible execution |
| Why this company? | Relevant operating experience and a clear productivity mission |

## 3. Homepage: explain the problem

### Section 2 — Why we do it

**Suggested heading**

Your people spend too much time holding the process together.

**Suggested copy**

Your organization already has software, data, and experienced people. Yet completing a task can still mean searching several systems, copying information, asking colleagues, checking documents, and coordinating the next step.

As demand grows, that work becomes a constraint on capacity, speed, and consistency.

Show three recognizable situations:

| Situation | Operational consequence |
|---|---|
| Information is scattered across applications and documents | People wait, search, and depend on specialists |
| Employees repeatedly transfer and reconcile information | Work takes longer and mistakes create rework |
| Every additional customer or case adds similar manual effort | Growth puts pressure on staffing and coordination |

Then introduce the opportunity:

**Let AI agents carry more of this repeatable work, using the knowledge and systems your organization already owns.**

This gives Core Agentic a clear purpose: improving how work moves through an organization.

## 4. Homepage: establish productivity and economics

### Section 3 — What organizations can gain

**Suggested heading**

More work completed. Better operating economics.

Organize the value proposition into five outcomes.

| Outcome | What it means in practice | What we measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Productivity** | Less human effort spent gathering, processing, and moving information | Accepted tasks per team hour |
| **Agility** | Faster answers, preparation, and response to changing situations | Turnaround and waiting time |
| **Accuracy and consistency** | Fewer avoidable omissions, transcription mistakes, and inconsistent steps | Error, rework, and acceptance rates |
| **Transparency** | Clearer visibility into sources, actions, progress, and exceptions | Traceability and exception handling |
| **Economics** | Greater capacity and lower cost per completed task | Total cost per accepted outcome |

### Make capacity a prominent message

**Grow output without a matching increase in headcount.**

Explain that reducing repetitive effort can help an existing team handle more customers, reports, cases, or research assignments. People retain responsibility for judgment, relationships, and consequential decisions.

### Make the economic argument concrete

“AI is cheaper” becomes most persuasive when connected to the full cost of completing work.

**Suggested copy**

The opportunity is to reduce the cost of completing a task while maintaining the quality your organization requires. That means counting the work AI performs, the review people still do, and the cost of running the solution.

Use the illustrative example from the presentation:

| Human effort per task | Before | Illustrative AI-assisted process |
|---|---:|---:|
| Work suitable for automation | 60 minutes | 12 minutes |
| Other work | 40 minutes | 40 minutes |
| Additional review | — | 8 minutes |
| **Total** | **100 minutes** | **60 minutes** |

**Illustrative outcome: 40% less human effort, including review.**

The financial case then includes platform, integration, AI usage, support, and operating costs. Released capacity becomes valuable when the organization can use it productively.

Keep the assumptions visible and label the calculation as an illustration. Customer results should use measured baselines and accepted outputs.

## 5. Homepage: make the applications real

### Section 4 — Where Core Agentic works

**Suggested heading**

From customer-facing work to internal operations.

Use substantial examples that visitors can inspect. Each example should show the original task, relevant information, agent contribution, human checkpoint, and output.

### A. Inbound sales and service

**Business problem**

Inquiries arrive through different channels. Staff must gather requirements, consult service information, prepare a response, and coordinate follow-up.

**Example workflow**

An agent gathers the inquiry details, retrieves approved product or service knowledge, prepares a response or quotation inputs, and routes the case to the appropriate person or process.

**Potential value**

Faster response, more consistent preparation, and less repetitive coordination.

**Measures**

Response time, inquiry completeness, handling effort, and successful handoffs.

### B. Outbound sales and account research

**Business problem**

Teams spend substantial effort researching organizations, assembling account context, and preparing outreach.

**Example workflow**

An agent researches a target organization, combines permitted sources, prepares an account brief, and drafts a relevant next step for review.

**Potential value**

More researched accounts and better preparation from the same team capacity.

**Measures**

Research time, accepted briefs, information quality, and downstream sales outcomes.

### C. Internal operations and reporting

**Business problem**

Answering an operational question requires navigating databases, spreadsheets, documents, and business applications.

**Example workflow**

An employee asks a business question. The agent retrieves authorized information, performs the required analysis, identifies exceptions, and prepares a report or proposed follow-up.

**Potential value**

Faster access to operating information, less report preparation, and fewer manual transfers.

**Measures**

Preparation time, completeness, rework, and exception-resolution time.

### D. Research and knowledge-intensive work

**Business problem**

Useful information is distributed across sources and must be collected, compared, enriched, and checked.

**Example workflow**

An agent coordinates research tools and published workflows, follows relevant relationships, and produces a reviewable brief or structured output with supporting evidence.

**Potential value**

Greater research capacity and more consistent evidence gathering.

**Measures**

Accepted outputs, review effort, coverage, and turnaround.

### Evidence behind the examples

The repository provides concrete starting points for research workflows and warehouse/finance reporting. These are candidates for the first demonstrations.

Inbound and outbound sales examples should reflect the capabilities qualified for the proposed configuration. The DCI materials currently describe a solution design and implementation gaps.

Use clear labels such as **product demonstration**, **configured workflow**, or **customer implementation**, according to the evidence available. A customer story should explain what was actually delivered and measured.

## 6. Homepage: explain how we work

### Section 5 — Turn a valuable problem into a working solution

**Suggested heading**

Your problem. Your data. A working proof.

**Suggested introduction**

We work closely with your business and technical teams to identify a valuable bottleneck, build a focused solution, and evaluate its performance before expanding the scope.

This should become a distinctive part of Primitive AI’s offering.

### Proposed delivery process

| Stage | What we do together | What the customer receives |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Find the opportunity** | Identify the process, owner, workload, bottleneck, and business impact | A focused problem statement and initial value hypothesis |
| **2. Define the proof** | Agree the information, access, scope, review points, and success criteria | A bounded working plan and evaluation method |
| **3. Build alongside the team** | Configure agents, knowledge, connections, and workflows using approved data | A working proof around the selected process |
| **4. Evaluate the result** | Assess accepted outputs, human effort, quality, exceptions, and operating cost | Evidence for a decision to proceed, refine, or stop |
| **5. Put it into operation** | Complete production preparation, train users, assign ownership, and establish support | An operational workflow with clear responsibilities |
| **6. Expand from evidence** | Extend useful capabilities to adjacent work and additional teams | A growing organizational AI capability |

### What makes this approach distinctive

**Close collaboration with the people doing the work**

Our team learns the actual process, including exceptions and informal handoffs.

**A working proof using approved customer information**

The engagement makes feasibility and operating requirements concrete.

**Evaluation agreed before development**

Business and technical teams know what evidence will support the next decision.

**Reusable platform capabilities**

Customer-specific knowledge and configuration build on a maintained core.

**Employee enablement**

Training covers useful instructions, output verification, sensitive information, permissions, and escalation.

### First-engagement invitation

**Bring us a costly or time-consuming workflow.**

We will explore what makes it difficult, what an improved process could look like, and what would be required to test it.

**Action:** Plan a working session

The scope, preparation, duration, deliverables, and commercial terms should be agreed for each engagement.

## 7. Homepage: substantiate the solution

### Section 6 — What powers Core Agentic

**Suggested heading**

Agents that understand the work. Knowledge that supplies context. Tools that carry it out.

Now show the architecture in greater detail.

### Four capabilities to explain

**Orchestration**

Agents interpret requests, plan steps, select approved capabilities, inspect results, and ask for missing information.

**Organizational knowledge**

Approved documents, records, and relevant relationships give the agent context. Supporting sources remain available for inspection.

**Workflows and integrations**

Published workflows handle repeatable processing. Governed connections reach permitted operations in existing business systems.

**Control and visibility**

Permissions, approvals, execution limits, and visible history keep the work within organizational boundaries.

Use two strong visuals:

1. An architecture diagram showing distinct existing systems and the intelligence and execution layer connecting them.
2. An authentic product capture showing planning narration, tool activity, results, and points requiring attention.

Connect the knowledge story to the founder’s experience with response quality. Explain how source grounding and review improve dependability.

## 11. Commercial presentation

The economic discussion should connect value, scope, and cost.

Once commercially approved, explain the components:

- Deployment and integration.
- Recurring platform access.
- AI and third-party usage.
- Additional workflows, custom work, or support where needed.

Use the customer’s business case to assess whether the proposed scope makes economic sense. Contracted charges and responsibilities should remain understandable.

The Palantir-inspired delivery approach does not determine Primitive AI’s prices, contract lengths, or guarantees.
